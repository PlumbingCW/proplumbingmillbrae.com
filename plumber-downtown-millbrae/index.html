Plumbing for Downtown Millbrae

Downtown Millbrae has an official planning name. The city's 2040 General Plan and its Downtown and El Camino Real Specific Plan focus on the downtown near Millbrae Station, where BART, Caltrain and SamTrans meet. Broadway is the main commercial street, and El Camino Real runs alongside. The city's zoning code also has a Millbrae Station Area Planned Development district.

This is a mixed district, so we treat it differently from the residential neighborhoods on this site. Restaurants, shops and offices sit next to condos and a few older homes, and each has its own plumbing questions. Tell us on the phone whether the problem is in a home or a business, and which building.

Businesses and homes share the same streets

Real estate profiles describe the housing here as mostly condo communities, many from the 2000s, with some 1940s bungalows. A 278-unit apartment building with ground-floor retail has also been reported for El Camino Real, so the mix is still changing.

In a newer building, the plumbing is often newer too, but age alone does not decide anything. Fixtures wear out, drains clog and water heaters fail in any building. We look at what is actually there.

Restaurant and kitchen drains

Food service puts grease and food solids into drains every day. When a kitchen line slows, drain cleaning and, for stubborn buildup, hydro jetting can clear it.

Retail and office plumbing

Restrooms, break rooms and small kitchens add up. A running toilet or a dripping tap in a shared space is easy to ignore and easy to fix. Our commercial plumbing page lists what we handle.

Condos and apartments

Leaks in a ceiling or along a shared wall can start in a unit above or in a common line. Leak detection helps trace the source so the right party handles it.

Water heaters

Small businesses and multi-unit buildings often rely on a single heater. If hot water drops, call and tell us the make and age, and we will explain repair or replacement.

Tenants, landlords and associations

Downtown, many plumbing problems raise a question before the repair does. Who is allowed to call a plumber? A tenant may need the landlord's approval. A condo owner may need the association. A business tenant may need the property manager. We suggest checking before you book, and we will write down what we find so you can pass it along.

If you manage a building

Tell us the building, the unit and any shared lines affected. We will explain what we found and what the repair involves in terms you can forward to the owner or the association.

City services in the downtown area

Millbrae buys its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission and delivers it through the city system. The city also runs the sewer, and under city code the owner generally maintains the sewer lateral from the building to the main. For commercial buildings, additional rules may apply, such as grease interceptor and backflow requirements. Check the current requirements with the city and your health or building inspector.
