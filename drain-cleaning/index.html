While you wait, stop running water in the house, keep the washing machine and dishwasher off, and move rugs and electronics away from the wet floor. Do not pour chemical drain opener into a standing blockage, because it sits there and can burn the next person who opens the pipe.

If only one sink or tub is slow, you probably have a local clog in that fixture's trap or branch line. If several fixtures gurgle or back up together, the blockage is further down the line, and that changes the tools we bring.

Backed-up drain emergency: signs to act on right now

Most blockages announce themselves a few days before the drain stops completely. Knowing which sign you have tells us whether to start with a hand cable at the fixture or a larger machine at a cleanout.

One fixture drains slowly

Water pools in the sink or tub and leaves a little at a time. This is usually hair, soap film or food residue packed into the trap or the short branch behind it, and a cable at the fixture often clears it.

Gurgling in other fixtures

When you flush a toilet and the shower drain bubbles, air is being pushed through a partly blocked line. That points to the shared branch or the main drain, not to one fixture.

Washer water in the tub or floor drain

Laundry discharge that rises in a nearby tub or floor drain means the line downstream is restricted. The washer moves a lot of water quickly, so it shows a blockage before a sink does.

Water backing up after every use

If the kitchen sink fills when the dishwasher drains, grease has probably narrowed the line. Left alone it hardens further, and the next stop is a full blockage.

Repeat clogs in the same place

A drain that clears and then slows again within weeks is telling you about a belly, a joint offset, roots or scale in the pipe. A fresh cable pass will not fix that on its own.

What causes drains to clog in older Millbrae houses

Millbrae grew fast after the war. City counts show dwelling units climbing from 2,783 in 1950 to 4,803 in 1960, so a large share of the housing stock carries drain pipe that has been in the ground or the wall for decades. Older clay, cast iron and early plastic joints collect scale, roots and sediment more readily than a newer line, which is why a drain that has cleared once may need a camera look the second time.

Inside the house the usual culprits are simpler. Kitchen lines take grease and starch, bathroom lines take hair and soap, and laundry lines take lint that a mesh trap never stops. Wipes sold as flushable are a steady cause of toilet and main line stoppages, so treat them as trash.

How we clear a drain

We start by asking which fixtures are affected and when it began, because that narrows the search before anyone picks up a tool.

1

Find the blockage point

We run water, listen, and check the nearest cleanout. A single fixture points to the trap and branch, several fixtures point to the main drain, and that decides where we open the line.

2

Cable the line

A cable with the right head cuts through hair, grease and soft debris, and pulls out what it catches. For small lines this is quick and tidy, and we lay protection down before we start.

3

Camera when the answer is not obvious

If the clog returns or the cable hits something solid, we send a camera down the pipe to see roots, a crushed section or a joint that has shifted. Seeing the pipe replaces guessing about it.

4

Step up to jetting if needed

Grease and scale that a cable only punches through need to be scoured off the wall. We will explain when hydro jetting is the better tool, so you pay for the job the pipe actually needs.

5

Test and explain

We run water through the whole fixture, confirm it drains freely, and tell you what we found. If the cause is a fault in the pipe, you hear it from us in plain terms before anything else is proposed.

Your lateral, your responsibility

Under Millbrae Municipal Code chapter 8.20, the property owner builds, maintains and clears the sewer lateral on their own lot. The city looks after only the stretch between the cleanout wye and the main, and only where the cleanout sits in the designated spot, such as the sidewalk. So a blockage in the pipe that leaves your house is yours to clear, even when it feels like a street problem.

The same chapter says an owner must clear an obstruction immediately once they find it or the city notifies them. Roots are singled out too: the city can require root removal from a private lateral, with 120 days after notice to remove the roots and repair the pipe so it does not recur. A cable pass that only reopens the line for a month will not satisfy that.

Because the code also covers testing and repair of laterals at certain property sales, a drain history that includes repeat blockages is worth a look on camera. Ask us about a sewer camera inspection if the same drain keeps slowing down.

What people try first, and what we do instead

DIY or what people try

  • Pour liquid drain opener into a standing blockage
  • Push a plunger harder at a drain that is full of water from the main line
  • Run a short hardware store snake that stops at the first bend
  • Keep running the dishwasher and hope the clog gives way
  • Flush wipes or paper towels down the toilet to move the water

Professional approach

  • Locate the blockage before choosing a tool
  • Use a cable sized to the pipe so it does not kink or break off in the line
  • Pull a camera through when the clog keeps returning
  • Recommend jetting only when scale or grease justifies it
  • Explain the cause and what to change so it stays clear

Grease, laundry and kitchen drains

Kitchen drains deserve their own mention because grease is the cause people least suspect. Warm oil runs down the pipe and looks harmless, then cools into a coating that catches everything after it. Wipe pans with a paper towel before washing and keep a can for oil.

Millbrae's own code treats fats and oils seriously for businesses, setting a local limit of 100.0 mg/L of oil and grease in what goes to the city sewer. That rule is written for commercial kitchens, but it shows why a home line shared with a garbage disposal clogs faster than one without. If your kitchen sink and disposal back up together, our garbage disposal page covers that side of the problem.
