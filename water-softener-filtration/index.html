Water Softener and Filtration in Millbrae, CA

Millbrae water is on the softer side, so a salt softener is often optional here. Filtration for taste and chloramine is a different question, and we help you sort out which one you actually need.

We install and repair softeners, carbon filters, and whole-house systems, and we will tell you plainly when you can skip the equipment.

Before you buy a softener or a filter, find out what problem you are trying to solve. In Millbrae, the water most households get is already soft by common standards, so a lot of the hardness problems that drive softener sales in other parts of California do not show up the same way here.

That does not mean filtration is pointless. Some people want better taste, less chlorine smell, or protection for specific appliances. Others already own a system that has stopped working. We cover both, and we start with what your water actually is.

What is in Millbrae's water

The City of Millbrae buys its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, and the 2025 Millbrae water quality report shows the figures that matter for this decision.

The grains figure comes from our own conversion of the 62 ppm average, since the report lists parts per million only. The range matters too. A reading near the top of that range can leave more spotting than one near the bottom, so the water at your tap may sit above or below the average.

Do you actually need a softener

For most Millbrae homes, probably not, and that is not what some sellers will tell you. A salt softener exists to remove hardness minerals, and with an average of 62 ppm there is not much hardness to remove. Scale on faucets, spots on glass, and crusty showerheads can still appear, but usually in mild form.

When a softener can still make sense

If you see heavy scale on fixtures, your water heater shows mineral buildup when it is flushed, or you want softer feeling water for personal preference, a softener may still be reasonable. It is a comfort choice here, not a necessity. We will tell you if your situation does not justify the equipment.

If you are not sure, a simple hardness test strip at your tap gives you a rough number to compare with the 2025 report. That costs far less than a system, and it tells you whether the symptoms you are seeing are really hardness.

What filtration can and cannot do here

Filtration is where Millbrae's chloramine matters. Chloramine is the disinfectant the water system uses, and it does not boil off the way plain chlorine does. A basic carbon filter helps with taste and smell, but chloramine reduction usually calls for a catalytic carbon block or a good contact time, so the filter type matters.

Taste and smell

A carbon filter at the kitchen tap or under the sink is the most direct fix. It is far less expensive to install than a whole-house system and addresses what most people notice.

Whole-house carbon

Treats every tap, shower, and appliance. It helps if you notice the smell in showers or want to reduce chloramine throughout the house, and it requires proper sizing and cartridge changes.

Sediment pre-filter

Catches rust flecks and particles from older pipes. It does not change the water chemistry, but it protects fixtures and other filters downstream.

Drinking water systems

Under-sink or reverse osmosis units give the cleanest drinking and cooking water at one tap. They need an extra faucet and a drain connection.

If you keep fish, run a dialysis machine, or have another sensitive use, tell us. Chloramine matters for those, and generic filters may not be the right answer.

Repairing a system you already own

A lot of calls come from people who bought a system and it stopped working. Common problems include a control head that no longer regenerates, a salt bridge, a clogged resin bed, a leaking fitting at the bypass, or a filter that has become restrictive because the cartridge was never changed.

We can service water conditioners and filters, including units installed by someone else. If a system is old and the repair cost approaches the cost of a new unit, we tell you. Given Millbrae's softer water, we also tell you if replacing a failed softener is even worth it.

What drives water treatment cost

Costs vary widely between a single tap filter and a whole-house system.

System type

carbon filters, sediment housings, softeners, and reverse osmosis have different equipment costs.

Size and capacity

how many people and fixtures the system serves decides the tank or housing size.

Location and plumbing

an easy mounting spot with a nearby drain is simpler than a tight closet.

Bypass and extras

shutoff valves, pressure gauges, and extra faucets add materials.

Ongoing costs

cartridge changes and salt are recurring, so we talk through them before you buy.
