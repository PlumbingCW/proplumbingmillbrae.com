Faucet that will not shut off: urgent signs and common problems

Faucets fail in predictable ways. The tricky part is that the symptom does not always point to the failed part, and a repair that treats the wrong one wastes a visit.

Drip from the spout

A steady drip means the cartridge, seat or washer inside the faucet no longer seals. Many single-handle faucets can be rebuilt with a new cartridge, but a pitted body or a model that is no longer made is better replaced.

Leak around the handle or base

Water at the handle usually points to worn O-rings or a loose packing nut. Water pooling around the base of the faucet can come from the deck seal or from a leaking supply connection underneath.

Burst or broken supply valve

A valve under a sink or behind a toilet can crack, corrode or snap when someone turns it. Water sprays or runs steadily until the main is shut, which makes this the most urgent faucet-area call.

Shower valve or showerhead leak

A leak at the showerhead is often a worn washer or connection. Water inside a wall or behind the tub is different, and it needs the valve body opened up and inspected quickly.

Outdoor spigot that drips or will not turn off

A spigot that leaks behind the handle or inside the wall can soak the siding and the framing. If a hose cannot be removed or the handle will not shut off, call and tell us what you tried.

What happens during a faucet repair visit

We start with the shutoff, because a stuck or leaking valve changes the plan. If the supply valve under the sink will not close fully, we replace it first or shut the main, then work on the faucet itself.

1

Find the leak path

We dry the area, run the water and watch where it appears. Many "faucet leaks" turn out to be a loose supply nut or a failing valve below.

2

Repair or replace the part

If a cartridge, seat or O-ring fixes it, we replace that part. If the faucet body is worn or parts are hard to find, we explain why replacement saves money in the long run.

3

Test under pressure

We turn the water on, run hot and cold, check every connection and look under the sink again after a few minutes. Slow leaks often show up late.

Hot, cold, and why pressure matters in Millbrae

Millbrae gets all of its water from the San Francisco Regional Water System through a city utility network with 11 pressure zones. Pressure in the pipes is not the same everywhere, and a house on a higher-pressure zone puts more stress on cartridges, supply lines and shutoff valves.

In practice, if a faucet cartridge keeps failing or a supply line bursts more than once, the pressure at the house is worth checking before another part goes in. We can tell you whether a pressure regulator at the house makes sense. We will not tell you what a particular street runs at, because no public source gives that.

Millbrae water and your fixtures

Millbrae's 2025 Water Quality Report lists hardness from 8.1 to 112 ppm, with an average of 62. That is on the softer side, so heavy scale buildup in aerators and cartridges is less common here than in hard-water towns, though pockets of harder water can still leave white crust on spouts and showerheads.

The same report shows the water is disinfected with chloramine, so we choose replacement seals and cartridges made for everyday household water and check each seal when we put the faucet back together.

Plumbing work on a house can require a permit, and the Millbrae Building Division handles plumbing permits. A faucet swap or cartridge repair is routine. Moving a supply line or adding a fixture is a bigger job, and we tell you when it crosses that line.

DIY or what people try

  • Cranking the handle harder to stop a drip, which wears the seat
  • Wrapping tape on a leaking supply fitting and leaving it
  • Forcing a corroded shutoff valve and snapping it
  • Replacing a cartridge without checking the other end of the line

Professional approach

  • Identify the faucet model and match the correct cartridge or replace the unit
  • Replace a leaking fitting with the right washer or new line
  • Install a new shutoff valve before the faucet work begins
  • Test hot, cold and pressure after reassembly
