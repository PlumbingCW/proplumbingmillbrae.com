When a water heater leak is an emergency

Not every drip needs a midnight call, but some do. Water coming from the tank body, steady dripping from the relief valve pipe, or a wet floor that grows overnight deserves a same-day look. A leak from a loose fitting or a worn valve is often repairable, while a leak from the tank shell itself usually is not.

If the unit sits in a closet, garage or above living space, water travels quickly into drywall and flooring. We would rather you call early and turn the water off than wait to see whether it stops. Tell us the heater type, its approximate age and where the water is showing up, and we can plan the visit around it.

No hot water: what is usually behind it

Different heaters fail in different ways, and a few symptoms are easy to mix up. Here is what we check first.

Gas heater with no heat

A pilot that will not stay lit, a failed thermocouple or flame sensor, or a dirty burner can all leave you cold. These are often repairable parts, and the age of the tank decides whether it is worth fitting them.

Electric heater with no heat

Electric tanks rely on two heating elements and thermostats. A failed element or a tripped limit switch will leave you with lukewarm or cold water, and both are usually inexpensive parts.

Hot water that runs out fast

When the shower goes cold quickly, a lower element may have failed, or sediment has built up on the tank floor and is taking up room. A flush sometimes restores the supply, and a look at the burner or element tells us which.

Heat pump unit showing a fault

Hybrid and heat pump water heaters add a compressor, fan and condensate line to the usual tank. A blocked condensate drain, restricted airflow or a control fault can stop heat, and a quick check often finds it.

Water too hot or not hot enough

A thermostat set wrong, a failed mixing valve or a crossed connection can all give odd temperatures. These are different problems with different fixes, so we test before we replace anything.

How we diagnose and repair

We keep it simple and we show you the fault before we fix it.

1

Make it safe

We check for water, gas smell and electrical risk first. If the tank is leaking badly we shut the supplies and stop the water before anything else.

2

Find the real fault

We test the thermostat, element or burner, the relief valve and the connections, and we look at the tank for corrosion at the base and fittings. A leak at a fitting and a leak from the tank wall are very different jobs.

3

Tell you the options

If a part will fix it, we say so and price it. If the tank has failed or the repair would cost more than it is worth, we explain why and offer water heater replacement as the next step.

4

Repair and test

We fit the part, restore power or gas, and confirm the heater reaches temperature with no leaks. We also check that the relief valve discharge pipe is in place and that the seismic strapping is intact.

Millbrae water and your heater

Millbrae's water comes entirely from the San Francisco Regional Water System, which is mostly Hetch Hetchy water. The 2025 water quality report lists hardness between 8.1 and 112 ppm with an average of 62 ppm, which is on the soft side by common standards. For a tank that means scale is a slower problem here than in hard water towns, but sediment still collects over the years and is worth flushing.

The same report confirms the water is disinfected with chloramine. We mention it because it is part of what flows through your heater every day, and we can talk through what that means for your particular tank and fittings if you are weighing a repair against a new unit.

California law also requires that water heaters be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement, under Health and Safety Code section 19211. If we open up the heater area and find loose or missing straps, we will point it out and offer to correct it. The San Andreas Fault lies within a mile of the western edge of the city, so this is a practical safeguard and not a formality.

Things homeowners try and what we do differently

DIY or what people try

  • Relight a gas pilot repeatedly without finding out why it went out
  • Turn the thermostat to its highest setting to chase hot water
  • Ignore a damp patch under the tank until the floor softens
  • Drain the tank with the power still on, which can burn out an electric element
  • Cap or plug the relief valve pipe to stop a drip

Professional approach

  • Test the part that actually failed before ordering anything
  • Check the thermostat range and mixing valve for scald risk
  • Look at the tank base and fittings for the source of moisture
  • Switch off power or gas, then drain and flush properly
  • Leave the relief valve and its discharge pipe clear so pressure can escape safely
