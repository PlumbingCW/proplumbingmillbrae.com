Tankless Water Heater Service in Millbrae, CA

A tankless water heater has no storage tank. It fires a burner or heating element only when you open a hot tap, and it heats the water as it passes through. That is why the supply does not run out in a long shower, and why the unit takes up so little room on a wall.

The same design means the unit works harder at each use. Scale, gas supply, venting and flow all matter more than on a tank, and small problems show up as error codes, lukewarm water or short bursts of cold. Getting those details right at installation and keeping up with maintenance is what makes a tankless heater last.

Why tankless units need descaling

Water carries dissolved minerals, and heat makes them settle out as scale on the inside of the heat exchanger. In a tank the scale falls to the bottom, but in a tankless unit it narrows the small passages the water flows through. The result is less flow, higher temperatures inside the exchanger, noisy operation and eventually an error code or shutdown.

A flush removes that scale. We connect pumps and hoses to the unit's service valves, circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger, rinse it, and check the inlet filter screen. Most manufacturers recommend doing this regularly, and the right interval depends on your water and use.

Hardness, chloramine and what Millbrae water means for your unit

Millbrae's water comes from the San Francisco Regional Water System. The city's 2025 water quality report lists hardness between 8.1 and 112 ppm, with an average of 62 ppm, which is on the soft side by common standards. For a tankless unit that is good news, since scale forms more slowly than in hard water areas, but it does not mean the heat exchanger never needs a flush.

The same report notes that the water is disinfected with chloramine. If you are thinking about adding a softener or filter to protect a tankless unit, the choice should fit that water, and our water softener and filtration page explains what we can set up. We would not recommend a softener just to recommend one, and with water this soft many homes do not need it.

Signs your tankless heater needs attention

Water runs lukewarm

If the unit never reaches the set temperature at a normal flow, scale in the heat exchanger, a partly blocked inlet screen or a low gas supply may be to blame.

Error code on the display

Codes point to specific faults, such as ignition, flame sensing, venting, water flow or temperature. We read the code, check the cause behind it and do not just clear it and walk away.

Short bursts of cold water

When hot water cuts out and returns, the flow may be too low to trigger the unit, or the unit may be cycling on and off because of scale or a sensor.

Noise from the unit

Banging, rumbling or whistling often follow scale, restricted flow or a venting issue. It is worth a look before it becomes a failure.

Leaks at or under the unit

Water at the connections, the condensate drain or the bottom of the unit needs attention soon. A small leak can come from a loose fitting, but it can also come from inside the heat exchanger.

How we service and install tankless units

1

Read the unit and the history

We start with the model, the age, any error codes and what you have noticed. That tells us where to look first.

2

Check the supplies

We check the gas line size and pressure, the water flow, the inlet filter screen, the vent and the electrical connection. A tankless unit is only as good as what feeds it.

3

Flush or repair

For a flush we circulate descaling solution, rinse and check the result. For a repair we replace the failed part, then test the unit through several cycles.

4

Install or swap with care

For a new unit, we size the gas line, plan the vent, fit isolation valves for future flushes and set the temperature. If you are replacing a tank, we explain what changes in the wall and the venting.

Millbrae homes and tankless conversions

A large share of Millbrae's housing dates from the postwar boom that took the city from 2,783 dwelling units in 1950 to 4,803 in 1960. In older homes the gas line serving the old tank is often sized for a smaller appliance, and a tankless unit can demand a lot more gas at once. Checking the line size is the first step before we quote a conversion.

Bay Area rules on new water heaters are changing, including rules that reach tankless units, so ask us what applies to your installation before you buy. Plumbing permits go through the Millbrae Building Division, and permits may apply to a new water heater, so we confirm what your job needs.

California also requires water heaters to be braced or strapped against earthquake movement. A wall-hung tankless unit is lighter than a full tank, but we follow the same safety approach and mount it to the structure properly.

What people try and what works

DIY or what people try

  • Ignore an error code and reset the unit repeatedly
  • Run a descaling solution through without isolation valves or proper pumps
  • Add a tankless unit to a gas line that was sized for a tank
  • Turn the temperature up to fix lukewarm water
  • Skip the inlet screen when cleaning

Professional approach

  • Read the code and test the part it points to
  • Flush with proper pumps and rinse thoroughly
  • Check gas line size and pressure before installing
  • Test flow and temperature rise over several cycles
  • Clean the inlet screen and confirm clear flow
