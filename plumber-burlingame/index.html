Burlingame is a city of about 31,000 people (2020 Census), with a long-established downtown around Burlingame Avenue, a second shopping district along Broadway, and a Caltrain station that gives the city its center of gravity. Plumbing trouble here looks a lot like it does across the border, with a few differences that come from older housing and the way the city runs its own water and sewer.

Why older Burlingame homes tend to have emergency calls

Burlingame's median home was built in 1957, according to American Community Survey figures, and close to 30 percent of its housing units date from 1939 or earlier. That is older than Millbrae's median of 1962. For you, it means original supply lines, aging drain pipes and tired shut-off valves are more likely to be in the walls and under the floors.

Age alone does not mean a pipe is about to fail, but it does explain the calls we hear most often. Here is how those problems usually show up.

A burst or spraying pipe

Water pouring from a wall, ceiling or crawlspace is the clearest emergency there is. Older supply lines can let go at a joint or a corroded stretch with little warning, and the water keeps running until the main is closed.

If you can reach the main valve, close it and then call the 24/7 line. We handle this under burst pipe repair.

A backed-up main drain

When a toilet gurgles, a tub fills with water that was not there before, or several fixtures slow down at the same moment, the main line is usually the culprit. Tree roots and old pipe joints are common causes in established neighborhoods.

Stop using water in the house and call. This is the kind of job where sewer line cleaning gets the flow moving again.

No hot water, or a leaking tank

A tank that drips at the base, rumbles loudly, or leaves you with cold showers is on borrowed time. A leak from the tank itself can turn into a flooded utility closet or garage.

Turn off the cold water supply to the heater and the power or gas control, then call us about water heater repair.

A hidden leak you cannot find

Stained ceilings, a warm patch on the floor, or a meter that keeps moving when everything is off point to water escaping somewhere you cannot see. It rarely stays small.

We trace it with leak detection before opening anything up, so you are not paying for guesswork.

Burlingame's own water and sewer, and what it means at your door

Burlingame runs its own city Water Division, which buys water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission and delivers it through more than 100 miles of mains and six reservoirs. The city also serves Burlingame Hills, an unincorporated area next door. If the leak is on the street side of your meter, it is the city's concern. If it is past the meter, it is yours, and we can tell you which side you are looking at.

On the sewer side, Burlingame maintains its own mains, six lift stations and a wastewater treatment facility. The city's utility page describes sewer work covering mains and laterals, so before anyone digs, ask us to help sort out which stretch belongs to the city and which belongs to you.

Hot water from the Hetch Hetchy supply

Water from the SFPUC system is treated with chloramine, and the regional supply is not especially hard. For a Burlingame water heater, that usually means scale is a smaller worry than in hard-water towns, though old tanks still corrode from the inside. If yours is past its prime, a planned swap beats a flooded closet at midnight.

Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer backup in Burlingame is an emergency because the water carries waste and can spread fast across a floor. Stop running every fixture, including the washing machine and dishwasher, because anything you drain adds to the overflow. Keep children and pets out of the area, and do not mix drain chemicals into standing water.

Then call the 24/7 line. We will ask where the backup shows first, which tells us whether the problem sits in a single drain or in the main. A single slow sink often responds to drain cleaning, while a backup at the lowest fixture in the house usually points further down the line.

Before we arrive

Shut off the main water valve

Find the main shut-off, usually where the line enters the house or near the meter, and turn it clockwise until it stops. If it will not budge, do not force it. Tell us when you call and we will talk you through the next step.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, switch off the breaker for that area only if you can reach it without standing in water. If you cannot do that safely, stay out and say so on the phone.

Clear the area

Move rugs, furniture, electronics and anything valuable away from the water, and keep children and pets out. Open a path from the front door to the problem so the plumber can work right away.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell us what you see, where the water is, and whether it is still running. If you smell gas, leave the building first and call 911 and your gas utility from outside, then call us once you are safe.
