Plumbing for Millbrae Highlands homes

Millbrae Highlands is the name many Millbrae residents and real estate listings use for the foothill neighborhood near Central Park. No official city map draws its edges, so we do not pretend to know exactly where it starts and stops. If you are not sure whether your street counts, call and give us the address. That is all we need to plan the visit.

What sets the Highlands apart is the mix of ages. Listings describe early 20th-century cottages, Tudor and Spanish-style houses, with construction going back to 1927, and newer homes higher up the slope that often have bay views. Most of the homes are single-family houses, so most calls involve one household and one set of pipes.

Why the age of the house matters, and why it is only a clue

A house from the 1930s and a house from the 1970s can sit a few blocks apart here. Age tells us where to look first. It does not tell us what is wrong. We do not assume a home has a particular pipe material or a particular problem just because of its year. We look at what is actually in the walls, under the floor and in the yard.

That is why the first step on most calls is a conversation. Tell us what you see, hear and smell, and how long it has been going on.

A slow or backed-up drain

One slow sink is usually local to that fixture. When several fixtures gurgle or the lowest drain fills first, the cause may sit further down the line. Drain cleaning clears the blockage, and a camera can show what caused it.

A leak you cannot place

A damp spot, a musty smell or a water bill that climbs with no new habit all point to hidden water. Leak detection finds the source with instruments before anyone cuts into a wall.

A water heater that has stopped working

No hot water, a leaking tank or rumbling noises are the usual signs. We compare repair against replacement and explain both so the choice is yours.

Remodel and fixture work

Older homes get updated one room at a time. New bathrooms and kitchens change what the drain and supply lines must carry, and some of that work needs a permit.

Water, sewer and what belongs to you

Millbrae buys all of its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission and delivers it through the city system. The city also runs its own sewer system. Under Millbrae Municipal Code chapter 8.20, the property owner generally maintains the sanitary sewer lateral, the pipe from the house to the main. The city maintains the main and, in some cases, a short piece of the lateral from the cleanout.

For a Highlands homeowner, the takeaway is simple. A problem in the street is the city's concern, and a problem on your side of the connection is yours. If you are unsure which applies, a call to the city's Public Works department settles it, and we can tell you what we see on your side.

Selling or remodeling an older house

The city code describes a lateral test when a property with buildings more than 20 years old changes hands, and a camera inspection when a permit adds plumbing fixtures or enlarges the building by more than 25 percent. How these rules are applied can change, so confirm the current requirement with the city before you list or file. A sewer camera inspection shows the condition of the lateral.

How to prepare before we arrive

Shut off the water

Close the main valve if water is running. If you are not sure where it is, tell us on the phone and we will help you find it.

Keep the area clear

Move furniture, rugs and valuables away from wet floors, and keep children and pets out of the room.

Note what you saw

When it started, which fixtures are affected and what changed just before. These details save time.
