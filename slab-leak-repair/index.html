If you suspect a leak under the floor, shut off the main water valve first. The line is open 24/7, so call as soon as the water is off. Then stay off wet floors near outlets or appliances, move rugs and boxes off the damp area, and leave the main valve closed until we have a plan.

A slab leak is a leak in a pressurized water line that runs in or beneath a concrete slab. Because the pipe is buried, the first clue is usually indirect: a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water with everything off, a water meter that keeps turning, or damp carpet along a wall.

When a slab leak is an emergency

Not every slab leak floods the house on day one, but the water has to go somewhere. It saturates the soil under the slab, wets the base of walls, lifts flooring, and feeds mold. A small leak you ignore for a few weeks can become a much bigger repair.

Treat it as urgent right now if you see any of these:

  • Water pooling or seeping up through the floor, a baseboard, or a slab crack
  • A hot spot on the floor you can feel through your shoes, which points to a hot water line
  • The meter dial or low-flow indicator moving with every fixture off
  • A sudden drop in water pressure at several fixtures at once
  • The sound of rushing or hissing water coming from the floor or a wall

We take after-hours calls for exactly this. A leak that starts at night can soak flooring by morning, so closing the main and calling the same night is the right order.

Signs the leak is under the slab

Warm or hot floor spots

A hot water line leaking into the slab heats the concrete above it. The warm patch usually follows the pipe route, which helps us narrow the search.

A meter that never stops

With every fixture and appliance off, a moving meter means water is escaping somewhere. A slab leak is one of several possible sources, so we rule out toilets and outdoor lines first.

Damp or buckling flooring

Wood floors cup, laminate lifts, tile grout darkens, and carpet stays damp along one wall. The pattern often leads back to the leak.

Sound and smell

Hissing at night, or a musty odor that returns after you clean, can point to water that has been feeding the floor for a while.

How we find and fix a slab leak

1

Shut off and confirm

We confirm the main is closed, check the meter for movement, and rule out a toilet, a water heater, or an outdoor line as the source.

2

Locate before cutting

Acoustic listening and thermal tools pinpoint the leak. Cutting blind is how a small repair turns into a large patch, so we mark the spot first.

3

Explain the options

We lay out a spot repair through the slab, a reroute of the line through walls or attic, or a repipe of the affected section. You see the tradeoffs before choosing.

4

Repair and test

After the repair we pressure test the line, restore water, and check the meter again to confirm the leak is gone.

5

Close out the floor

We tell you what finish work comes next and which drying and flooring steps to line up with your insurance or contractor.

Local conditions that affect slab plumbing in Millbrae

Millbrae specifics for floor leaks

All of Millbrae's drinking water comes from the San Francisco Regional Water System, delivered through 11 pressure zones. Pressure differs from one zone to the next, and a line that runs at higher pressure works harder against every weak spot. If you have a slab leak and strong pressure at your taps, a pressure check on the house side is worth doing while we have the line open.

The ground matters too. The San Andreas Fault lies within a mile of Millbrae's western city limits, and the city's own hazard planning identifies landslide and liquefaction zones inside the city. Ground movement can stress buried lines, but we cannot tell you which streets are affected, so we judge each house by the leak we find, not by the map.

Housing age also plays a part. A large share of Millbrae's homes were built around the 1950s, so the original supply lines under or around the floor may be old. If we find one leak in an aged line, we tell you honestly whether more are likely and whether a repipe makes more sense than another patch.
