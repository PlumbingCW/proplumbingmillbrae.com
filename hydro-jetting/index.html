When hydro jetting is an emergency, and when it is the planned fix

Jetting is not the first tool we reach for on every clog. A cable or drum machine clears most single-fixture stoppages, and our drain cleaning service covers those. Jetting becomes the right call when the main line keeps backing up after being snaked, or when the blockage is a coating of grease or a mat of fine roots that a cable only punches a hole through.

Urgent cases are the ones where more than one drain is affected at once. A toilet that gurgles when the washer drains, a tub that fills when you flush, or a floor drain that bubbles all point to the shared line outside the house. Those are same-day jobs, and we take them after hours and overnight as well.

Water coming up in the lowest drain

The lowest fixture in the house is the first place a blocked main shows itself, usually a tub, shower or floor drain. If it happens as soon as another fixture drains, the stoppage is downstream of both.

A cable that only partly worked

Drains that clear for a week or two and then slow down again usually have a narrow channel through a larger obstruction. A cable cuts that channel but leaves the coating on the pipe walls, so it closes up again.

Grease buildup from the kitchen side

Cooled cooking fat sticks to the pipe wall and layers up over time. Hot water alone moves it a few feet and lets it set again further down the line.

Fine root growth in the lateral

Hair-thin roots enter at joints and spread into a mat that catches paper and waste. Jetting with the right nozzle cuts it back, and the camera shows us whether the joint itself needs repair.

Scale and sludge in older cast iron or clay

Rough pipe interiors hold debris and shrink the usable opening. Scouring restores flow, but only if the pipe wall underneath is still sound.

How a jetting visit runs

We do not start the jetter blind. A pipe that is cracked, offset or collapsed can be damaged by high pressure, so the first step is finding out what the line looks like inside.

1

Locate the access point

We find the cleanout or the nearest clean entry to the main. If there is no cleanout, we say so plainly, because that changes how the job is set up and what we recommend afterward.

2

Camera the line first

A small camera shows the blockage type, the pipe material and any damage ahead of the nozzle. This is where we decide between jetting, a cable, or stopping and talking about repair.

3

Scour with the right nozzle

Water pressure and nozzle type are matched to the pipe and the blockage. We work from the blockage back toward the house so debris flows to the main and does not pile up against the fixtures.

4

Camera it again

A second pass shows the pipe walls after cleaning. You get to see the result and any cracks or sags the debris was hiding.

5

Talk through what we found

We explain what caused the blockage and whether it is likely to return. If the pipe needs repair, we say where and why before anything else is booked.

What older Millbrae homes mean for jetting

Millbrae grew fast after World War II. Census counts show dwelling units rising from 2,783 in 1950 to 4,803 in 1960, and a large share of the city's housing dates from that boom. Lines laid in that era are often older clay or cast iron, and those materials can be rough inside and prone to root entry at joints. So a camera check before jetting is a practical necessity here, not an extra.

City rules also make roots your problem to solve. Under Millbrae's municipal code, the owner is responsible for the sewer lateral on their own lot, and the city can require root removal and repair within 120 days of notice.

Jetting clears roots from the bore, but it does not stop them returning through an open joint. If the camera shows root entry at a joint, a repair plan is the lasting fix, and we can walk through the options with you.

Millbrae specifics that affect your main line

Millbrae's sewer collection system is owned by the city and flows to its own Water Pollution Control Plant, but the lateral from your house to the main is yours to maintain. The city looks after only the stretch between the cleanout wye and the main, and only when the cleanout sits in the designated spot. If the stoppage is on your side, you or a plumber you hire clears it.

Businesses have an extra reason to care. Millbrae's code sets a local limit of 100 mg/L for oil and grease in wastewater, and lets the city specify the grease trap type and size for a business. A kitchen line that needs repeated jetting is often telling you the trap or the cleaning schedule is not keeping up.

When a blockage cannot be cleared by the owner or a hired contractor, the code expects it to be reported to Public Works. Costs the city incurs can be billed back to the owner, which is a good reason to call a plumber early.

Jetting compared with a cable

Cable or snake alone

  • Cuts a channel through the blockage and leaves the pipe wall coated
  • Works well for a single soft clog at one fixture
  • Can snag or coil in offset or broken pipe without showing you why
  • Often needs repeating when grease or roots are the cause

Hydro jetting with a camera

  • Scours the full circumference of the pipe wall
  • Handles grease, fine roots, scale and sludge in one pass
  • Camera before and after shows pipe condition and damage
  • Gives a clear basis for deciding on repair or a maintenance schedule
