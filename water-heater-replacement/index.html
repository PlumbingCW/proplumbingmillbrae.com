If your water heater tank is leaking from its body or flooding the floor, call our 24/7 line now. Shut the cold water valve above the tank, switch off the breaker for an electric unit, and keep everyone away from standing water near outlets. A gas smell changes the order of calls: get everyone outside first, then phone 911 and your gas utility, and call us after that.

Once the water is stopped, you have a little time to think. Most failed tanks are replaced the same day or the next, and a short wait for the right unit is better than a rushed choice that does not fit your space or your gas and electrical supply.

Flooding from the tank: what to do right now

A tank that has corroded through cannot be repaired, because the steel shell is what holds the water. A steady stream or a puddle that returns after you dry it means the tank is finished, and the hot water supply to your house will be lost until it is replaced.

Use towels and a wet vacuum to keep water from reaching drywall, flooring and anything stored near the heater. Do not try to seal the leak with tape or putty, and do not drain a hot tank by hand. Tell the dispatcher the tank size, whether it is gas or electric, and where it stands, and we will line up the right unit before we arrive.

How to tell the tank is beyond repair

Some failures are fixable and some are not. These are the signs that point to replacement rather than a new part.

Water seeping from the shell

Moisture that appears on the sides or bottom of the tank, not at a fitting, comes from inside. Once the steel is perforated, no part can fix it.

Rust-colored hot water

Brown or orange water from hot taps only suggests the tank interior is corroding. If it comes with leaks or a rusted base, replacement is usually the next step.

A tank that is many years old

Tanks wear out. When a heater is well into its second decade and starts to leak or lose heat, putting money into repairs often does not make sense.

Rumbling, popping or loud banging

Heavy sediment can trap steam bubbles and make a tank noisy. A flush may help, but a tank that was never maintained and is also leaking should be replaced.

Repeated repairs

If you have replaced a thermostat, then an element, then a valve, the tank may be telling you it is finished. We will help you weigh the next repair against a new unit.

What happens during a replacement

Replacement is a planned sequence, even when it starts as an emergency.

1

Confirm what you have

We check the tank size, fuel type, venting or electrical supply, and the space around it. This tells us whether a straight swap will work or whether something must change.

2

Remove the old heater

We shut and drain the tank, disconnect the water, gas or power, and take the unit out. We lay protection down and carry the old tank away so you are not left with it.

3

Set the new heater

We position the new tank, reconnect the supplies with new flexible connectors, fit a relief valve and discharge pipe, and strap it against earthquake movement. Gas units are leak tested at the connections.

4

Test and walk you through it

We fill the tank, bring it up to temperature, check every joint for drips and show you the shutoff valves and thermostat. You leave knowing where everything is.

Permits, strapping and who may do the work

A replacement may need a permit. Plumbing permits come from the Millbrae Building Division, and nothing on the city's pages tells us whether a like-for-like swap is exempt, so we do not guess. We ask the division for your address first.

Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires bracing on water heaters, and Millbrae sits with the San Andreas Fault within a mile of its western edge. A new tank therefore goes in strapped to the wall framing, never freestanding.

If you hire anyone for water heater work, check their licence on the Contractor State License Board site. Plumbing is classification C-36, which includes equipment that heats water, and the $1,000 minor-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit.

Bay Area rules are changing for new units

Rules on new water heaters in the Bay Area are changing, and what you can buy and install may depend on them. We will tell you what applies to your replacement when you call, so you do not buy a unit that no longer fits. Ask us before you order a heater online.

If you are thinking about switching fuel, for example from gas to a heat pump unit, that is a bigger project than a like-for-like swap. It can involve electrical capacity, a drain for condensate and clearance for airflow, and it is better planned than rushed on a day with no hot water.

DIY or what people try

  • Order a heater online and hope it fits the space and fuel
  • Swap a gas tank without checking the vent and gas connector
  • Skip the earthquake straps to save time
  • Reuse old flexible connectors and a worn relief valve
  • Leave the old tank in the garage for later disposal

Professional approach

  • Confirm size, fuel, venting and clearances before the unit arrives
  • Replace connectors, relief valve and discharge pipe with the tank
  • Strap the new tank as California requires
  • Test for gas and water leaks before leaving
  • Remove and dispose of the old tank

What affects the cost of a replacement

We give you the price for your specific job over the phone, so you can decide before work starts.

Size and type of tank

A larger tank or a heat pump unit costs more than a standard 40 or 50 gallon tank.

Fuel and venting

Gas units need a proper vent and gas connection, and a poor existing vent adds work.

Access

A heater in an attic or tight closet takes longer to remove and set.

Code items

Straps, relief valve discharge, expansion control and permits may add to the job.

Time of day

Nights and weekends can affect pricing, and we say so before dispatch.
