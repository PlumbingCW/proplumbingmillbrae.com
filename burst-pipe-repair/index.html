When a burst pipe is an emergency

A pipe that is dripping is a repair. A pipe that is spraying, flowing steadily, or pouring through a ceiling is an emergency, because water in the walls and floor spreads quickly and the damage compounds the longer it runs.

Treat it as urgent right now if you see any of these:

  • Water spraying or streaming from a visible pipe
  • A ceiling that is bulging, dripping, or stained and growing
  • Water coming through a wall or a light fixture
  • A flooded cabinet, garage, or crawlspace with no clear source
  • A sudden loss of water pressure alongside the sound of running water

We take overnight and weekend burst pipe calls, because pipes do not wait for a convenient hour. We confirm the main is off, locate the failure, and plan the repair so you are not left with a closed valve and no water for longer than you need.

What makes a pipe fail

Age and corrosion

Older metal supply lines thin out from the inside, develop pinholes, and eventually split. This is the most common cause of a burst in a house with original piping.

High water pressure

A line that runs at high pressure for years works against every joint. A failed pressure regulator or an unusually strong supply can finish off a weak spot.

Ground and structural movement

Settling, shifting soil, and earthquakes can pull a pipe at a fitting or crack a buried section. Even small movement strains a rigid line.

Freezing and thaw

Water expands when it freezes and splits the pipe wall. We see it less often than corrosion, but cold snaps still cause splits in exposed lines.

Damage from work

A nail, a trench, or a drilled stud can nick a pipe. The leak may show up days after the work.

How we handle a burst pipe

1

Stop the water

We confirm the main is closed, drain the line, and make sure the house is safe to work in.

2

Find the failure

We locate the burst and check the pipe on either side of it. A single failure often has weak neighbors.

3

Repair or replace

For a clean failure in sound pipe, we cut out the damaged section and splice in new pipe. If the line is corroded or cracked in several places, we replace a longer run.

4

Test and restore

We pressure test the repair, bring water back slowly, and check for hammer or secondary leaks.

5

Leave you ready for the next step

We tell you what we found, what to photograph for your insurer, and what drying and wall repair should come next.

Spot repair or a longer replacement

DIY or what people try

  • Wrap the break with tape or a rubber patch and leave it
  • Push a push-fit coupling on a corroded pipe
  • Turn the water back on to see if it holds
  • Cut out the damaged piece and glue in new pipe without checking the rest

Professional approach

  • Shut off, drain, and expose the full failure before cutting
  • Cut back to sound pipe and join with the right fitting for the material
  • Pressure test before water goes back on
  • Check nearby pipe and the pressure at the house, then explain whether a longer replacement makes sense

Temporary patches are fine for stopping a leak while you wait for us. They are not fixes. A repair clamp on corroded pipe may hold for a day and fail under load, and the second failure usually happens in a worse place.
