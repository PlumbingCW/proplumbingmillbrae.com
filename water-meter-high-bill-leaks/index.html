A high water bill is one of the most common signs of a hidden leak, and the meter is the cheapest tool for confirming it. Before you call anyone, you can do a quick test that tells you whether water is leaving your property when nothing should be running.

Water and sewer service in Millbrae is provided by the City of Millbrae Public Works Department, so your bill comes from the city. That also means the city is the right place to ask about billing questions, while we handle finding and fixing the leak on your side.

How to check your meter for a leak

You do not need special tools. You need about twenty quiet minutes and a clear view of the meter.

1

Turn everything off

Shut off every faucet, stop the washer and dishwasher, and make sure nobody flushes a toilet. Turn off irrigation as well.

2

Read the meter

Locate the meter box, lift the lid, and look for the low-flow indicator, a small dial or triangle that spins when any water moves. Note the numbers on the register.

3

Wait and look again

Wait fifteen minutes without using water and check again. If the indicator is moving or the numbers advanced, water is escaping.

4

Narrow it down

Close the main valve at the house and check the meter again. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the valve.

This test splits the problem in two, which speeds up our work. If you try it and the numbers moved, call us with what you saw.

Where a high bill usually comes from

Toilets that run or leak

A worn flapper lets water seep into the bowl without a sound. It can waste a lot of water over a month and rarely triggers an alarm.

Hidden pipe leaks

Leaks inside walls, under floors, or in crawlspaces can run for weeks. A moving meter with every fixture off is the signature.

Underground service line

A leak between the meter and the house shows up on the meter but never inside the home. See water service line repair for how we handle that.

Irrigation and sprinklers

Cracked heads, broken valves, and buried lines leak without anyone noticing, especially when timers run before dawn.

Water heater and appliance leaks

A relief valve that drips, a failing connector, or a leaking washer hose adds to the bill gradually.

Irrigation leaks and backflow devices

Irrigation is easy to forget because it runs on a schedule and the water goes into the soil. A buried leak keeps the zone wet and the meter moving, and the first sign is often a soggy patch or a lawn that grows unevenly.

Backflow assemblies on irrigation are covered by Millbrae's Backflow and Cross-Connection Control Program, which requires testing when installed and once a year at the owner's expense. The water provider can cut service if a required assembly is missing or untested, so a locked valve or a shutoff notice may be about testing rather than a leak. We can look at the assembly and the line together.

Millbrae specifics for bills and meters

All of Millbrae's water comes from the San Francisco Regional Water System, and the city serves about 6,600 accounts through 11 pressure zones. A higher-pressure zone pushes more water through any small opening, so the same crack wastes more gallons at your address than it might elsewhere. We check the pressure at your house while we are tracing the leak.

A large share of Millbrae's homes date to the 1950s, based on Census counts of dwelling units, so toilets, valves, and supply lines may be original or only partly updated. Older fittings are a common source of slow leaks that never make a mess but show up in the bill.

The city also publishes an annual water quality report with contact details for Public Works. If your bill spikes and you have no leak, ask Public Works whether the meter read is correct before assuming a plumbing problem.
