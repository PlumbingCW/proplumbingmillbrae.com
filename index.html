What to know about older Millbrae plumbing

What to know about older Millbrae plumbing

Millbrae grew fast after the war. Census counts show dwelling units rising from 2,783 in 1950 to 4,803 in 1960, so a large share of the homes in the city are more than six decades old. We cannot tell you what is inside your walls without looking, but a house from that era is a good candidate for a leak check when a ceiling stain or a rising water bill turns up.

Around Downtown Millbrae and El Camino Real, the questions are different. Restaurants and shops there deal with grease, floor drains and annual backflow testing, which the city's code requires wherever backflow assemblies are installed. Those are everyday jobs for commercial plumbing.

Near the Millbrae Station area, newer buildings replaced the old surface parking lots, and the city's hazard planning notes that parts of the station area sit in flood hazard zones. For a ground-floor unit or garage, that is a reason to ask about drain and backwater protection before the wet season, not after.

Hillside lots in the western part of town are a different story. The city's 2040 General Plan notes past landslides on steeper slopes there, and moving ground can stress buried water and sewer lines, so a soggy patch in a yard deserves a prompt look.
