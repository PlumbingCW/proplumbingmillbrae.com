Plumbing for Green Hills, Millbrae

Green Hills is one of the best-known place names in Millbrae. The Green Hills Country Club opened in 1930 and took its current name in 1945, according to a local history in Patch. Green Hills Park sits at Magnolia Avenue and Ludeman Lane, and Green Hills Elementary School carries the same name. Central Park is just to the south.

The city does not publish an official map of the neighborhood, so we describe it by these landmarks instead of by exact boundaries. If you are not sure whether you are in Green Hills, tell us your street when you call.

Why the housing mix changes the plumbing answer

Real estate profiles describe a mix here. There are single-family split-level homes and bungalows, many with Craftsman details, along with townhouses and condos. That mix matters because the first question on any call is not just what broke, but whose pipe it is.

In a detached house, almost everything from the meter to the fixtures is yours. In a townhome or condo, shared lines and a building shut-off may be managed by an association. Knowing which kind of home you have helps us explain the right next step.

A leak in a shared wall or ceiling

Water that appears in a ceiling or along a shared wall may come from your unit or from a neighbor's. Leak detection helps find the source, and your association can tell you what it is responsible for.

A backed-up drain

In a single house, a backup may point to your own lateral. In a shared building, it may point to a common line. Drain cleaning clears the blockage, and a camera shows where it sits.

Water heater problems

Houses usually have their own tank, and some condos share a system. We explain repair and replacement once we know your setup.

Toilets and fixtures

A running toilet or a dripping faucet wastes water every day. Toilet repair and installation and faucet work are routine jobs for any home type.

Who is responsible for which pipe

Millbrae buys its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission and delivers it through the city system. The city also runs its own sewer. Under city code, the owner of a property generally maintains the sewer lateral from the building to the main.

For a townhome or condo, the lateral and the water line may be shared, and the association's rules decide who pays. Read your governing documents or call the association manager. Then call us, and we will explain what we find so you can pass it along.
