A sewer camera inspection is the quickest way to learn the true state of the pipe between your house and the city main. A small camera on a flexible cable is fed in through a cleanout, and we watch the pipe on a monitor as it travels. Cracks, offsets, roots, grease and standing water show up on screen, and a locator on the surface marks where and how deep each one is.

People book it for different reasons. Some want an answer after repeated clogs, some are buying or selling a house, and some are about to remodel and need to know the pipe will survive the project. The video is the same in all three cases, but what you do with it differs.

What the camera can and cannot show

The camera shows the inside of the pipe, and it is very good at that. It cannot show how the pipe is supported in the soil outside it, and it cannot see past a full blockage. If a line is packed with debris, we may need to clear it first, and that is where sewer line cleaning comes in.

Root intrusion

Fine roots at joints appear as white threads or a thick mat. The camera shows whether the roots enter at a joint or through a crack, which decides the repair.

Cracks and breaks

A crack shows as a dark line, a broken pipe as an opening with soil behind it. We note the distance from the access point so the spot can be found again.

Offsets and sags

Joints that no longer line up, or low spots that hold water, show on the video. A pool of water in a section that should be dry is the sign of a belly.

Scale and corrosion

Older cast iron may be rough, flaking or narrowed. The camera shows how much opening remains and how thin the wall looks.

Grease and debris

Grease clings to the wall in a coating, and debris sits along the bottom. These point to a cleaning plan, not necessarily a repair.

How an inspection visit goes

1

Find the cleanout

We locate the cleanout near the house or the sidewalk. If the line has no cleanout, we tell you the best alternative and recommend one.

2

Run the camera

The camera travels toward the main while we watch and record. We note the pipe material, the joints and any defect along the way.

3

Mark depth and distance

A locator on the surface marks where the camera head is and how deep it sits. This tells us where a repair would need to be and what it would disturb.

4

Review the footage with you

We play back the video and show what each finding means in plain terms. You get the recording to keep for your records or your agent.

When a camera run is required in Millbrae

Millbrae's municipal code ties camera work to certain moments. Before a building permit that adds plumbing fixtures or alters more than 25% of a building's area, the code calls for a CCTV inspection of the lateral by a licensed plumber or sewer contractor. The pass criteria include no roots or obstructions, tight joints, cleanouts, a design life of 25 or more years, and a backwater valve and relief valve. Defects found are to be repaired within 90 days.

The code also has a rule for property sales. Where buildings are more than 20 years old, the lateral must be tested and repaired before title transfers, unless it passed a test within the past 5 years or was fully replaced within 20 years and the city accepted it.

That rule depends partly on a consent decree whose stated end date has passed, so confirm with Public Works whether it is being applied to your sale. The cost of testing and repair falls on the owner.

Millbrae specifics for inspection

Millbrae owns its sanitary sewer system and treats the flow at the city's Water Pollution Control Plant. Your lateral is yours from the house to the main, apart from the short piece between the cleanout wye and the main that the city maintains when the cleanout is in the designated location. A camera run shows whether trouble sits on your part of the line.

The city's own Sewer Modernization page describes the collection system as in need of significant repair and rehabilitation, and says it is inspecting pipes and house laterals. A recorded camera run on your lateral gives you a baseline of its condition if the city contacts you. Millbrae permits for plumbing work go through the Building Division, and permits may apply to repairs the footage leads to.

A large share of Millbrae housing dates from the 1950s boom, when dwelling units rose from 2,783 in 1950 to 4,803 in 1960. Laterals from that era are the ones most likely to show roots and joint trouble on camera.
