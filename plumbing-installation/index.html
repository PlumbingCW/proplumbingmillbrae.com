Most installation calls start the same way. Something is going in that was not there before: a second sink, a tub where a shower used to be, a washer hookup in a garage, a gas stove that needs a proper connection. The plumbing behind the wall decides how easy that is, so we begin by looking at what you already have.

Installation is different from repair because you control the timing. That gives you room to decide where lines run, what shutoffs you want, and which fixtures are worth buying before the walls close up.

What we install

The work falls into a few groups, and most jobs touch more than one of them.

Fixtures and faucets

Sinks, vanities, tubs, shower valves, and kitchen or bath faucets. A new fixture on an existing rough-in is a short job. A fixture in a new spot needs new supply and drain runs, which is where the real planning happens.

Appliance hookups

Washers, dishwashers, ice makers, water softeners, and similar equipment that needs a supply line, a drain, and often a shutoff you can reach without moving the machine.

New and moved lines

Water and drain lines for additions, converted garages, new bathrooms, and kitchen remodels. We route around framing, size the pipe for the fixtures it serves, and set the vent so the drain actually flows.

Tub and shower installation

Tub swaps, shower pans, and valve changes. The valve body and the drain are the parts you cannot fix later without opening the wall, so we settle them first.

Gas connections

Stoves, dryers, barbecues, and similar appliances. Gas work gets extra care and a leak check before we leave.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility before you call us.

How an installation job runs

1

Phone scope

You tell us what is going in, where, and what is already there. We ask about the age of the house, what the walls are made of, and whether a remodel or permit is already in motion.

2

Site check

We look at the shutoffs, the existing supply and drain lines, venting, and access. This is where most surprises show up, so we write down anything that changes the plan.

3

You get a clear price before work starts. If the job needs a permit, we say so up front and explain who pulls it and what the inspector will look at.

4

Install and test

We rough in, set fixtures, pressure test supply lines, and run every drain. Gas lines get a leak check.

5

Walkthrough

We show you the new shutoffs, label what we can, and leave the work area clean.

Permits and who is allowed to do the work

Plumbing work in Millbrae generally falls under city permit rules, and the Millbrae Building Division handles plumbing permits. Whether a particular job needs one depends on its scope, so permits may apply and we tell you what we see once we know what you are installing.

Hiring matters here too. The California Contractors State License Board lists C-36 as the plumbing classification, and you can look up any contractor's licence on the CSLB site before you sign. The state's small-job exemption, now $1,000, does not cover work that needs a permit, so a permitted bathroom or remodel is not something to hand to an unlicensed helper.

Millbrae specifics for installation jobs

Millbrae's housing stock grew fast in the 1950s. Census counts show dwelling units rising from about 2,800 in 1950 to about 4,800 in 1960, so a large share of local houses were built in that era. If your remodel opens a wall in one of them, expect to meet original supply and drain lines, and plan the new work to tie into them cleanly.

The sewer side matters too. Under Millbrae's sewer ordinance, a building permit that adds plumbing fixtures or alters more than 25 percent of the building area triggers a camera inspection of the sewer lateral by a licensed plumber or sewer contractor. A lateral that fails on roots, loose joints, or missing cleanouts has to be fixed within 90 days, so adding a bathroom can add a lateral repair to the schedule.

Some qualifying remodels also require a sewer backwater valve and a relief device on the lateral. That is a code item, not an upsell, and it is far cheaper to place it while the work is already open. Dig-in jobs outside the house, like a new yard line, need a USA North 811 ticket with at least two working days of notice before the shovel goes in.

The pattern is simple. If the fixture is the problem, we swap the fixture. If the pipe behind it is the problem, we fix the pipe once instead of dressing it up with a new faucet.

What drives installation cost
