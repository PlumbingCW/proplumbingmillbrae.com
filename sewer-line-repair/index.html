Sewer backup: what to do first, and why repair may follow

A backup that clears with a cable and returns in a few weeks is often a pipe problem, not a clog problem. Roots that entered a joint, a section that has sagged and holds water, or a crack that lets soil into the line will all bring the blockage back. Cleaning treats the symptom, and repair removes the cause.

That is why we treat repeated backups as urgent even when the first one seemed minor. The lateral also fails at the worst times, such as after heavy use on a weekend or late at night. We take those calls after hours, and a same-day visit is our goal when schedules allow.

Roots in the joints

Clay and older cast iron pipes have joints roots can enter. Cutting them back gives relief, but the opening stays unless the joint is repaired or lined.

Belly or sag in the line

A low spot holds water and waste, so paper settles and builds up. You see this on camera as a pool in a pipe that should drain dry.

Cracks and offsets

Ground movement, heavy loads or age can crack a pipe or shift a joint. A camera shows whether the damage is a clean break or a slow leak into the soil.

Corroded cast iron

Older cast iron can flake from the inside and narrow over time. Rough interior walls catch debris and the pipe may be thin where it is rusted.

Collapsed sections

A collapsed pipe cannot be cleared at all. It needs excavation or replacement, and the earlier it is found, the more options you have.

How a sewer repair job is planned

Every repair starts with seeing the damage. We do not recommend digging or lining until a camera has located the problem and measured how far it runs.

1

Camera and locate

A camera runs the line and a locator marks the depth and position of the damage on the surface. We record what we find so you can see it too.

2

Choose the repair method

Spot repair, trenchless lining or replacement each fit different damage. We explain why one suits your pipe better, what it costs in effort, and what it will do to the yard and driveway.

3

Plan permits and utilities

Plumbing permits may apply, and the Millbrae Building Division handles them. If digging is involved, a state utility locate ticket through 811 is required, with at least two working days of notice.

4

Do the work

We excavate or line the pipe, test the repair and restore the access we opened. Landscaping, concrete and paving finish work is a separate trade.

5

Confirm with a final camera

A camera pass after the repair shows the pipe is intact and flowing. You see the result before anything is buried or closed.

Millbrae's rules on the sewer lateral

In Millbrae the property owner builds, maintains and clears the sewer lateral on their own lot. The city maintains only the short piece between the cleanout wye and the main, and only if the cleanout is in the designated location. Everything else is your responsibility, so damage on your side is your repair.

The city code sets timelines that matter if you receive notice. After a notice of a leaking lateral, repair is due within 30 days. After a sewer overflow caused by a lateral, repair or replacement is due within 180 days, with exceptions for extensive remodels and hardship. If the city requires roots removed and the line repaired, the owner has 120 days. These are the city's rules, so confirm your dates with Public Works.

Millbrae specifics for repair planning

Millbrae's own sanitary sewer system flows to the city's Water Pollution Control Plant, and the city describes the collection system as in need of significant repair and rehabilitation. The city is inspecting and repairing pipes and house laterals under its Sewer Modernization Program. If you hear from the city about your lateral, a camera record from us is useful evidence of the pipe's condition.

The city code also calls for owner-paid sidewalk cleanouts and backflow or relief devices in listed situations, including new construction, remodels and lateral repairs. A backwater valve can keep sewage from flowing back into the house during a main blockage. We tell you whether your repair triggers either requirement before the work is booked.

A large share of Millbrae homes date from the mid-century building boom, and pipes of that age are the ones most likely to show roots, offsets and corrosion. A camera is the only way to know the condition of yours.
