When a buried water line is an emergency

A water service line leak does not always show up as a fountain. More often it is a soft patch of lawn, a sunken driveway edge, a damp corner of the garage, or water running at the street side of your property. Some leaks never surface, which is why the water bill or meter often gives them away first.

Treat it as urgent right now if you see any of these:

  • Water surfacing in the yard, driveway, or sidewalk
  • A meter that spins with every fixture off
  • No water or very weak pressure throughout the house
  • A wet foundation, crawlspace, or garage floor with no obvious source
  • A sudden jump in the water bill with no change in use

We answer buried line calls at night and on weekends. An active leak under pavement or near the foundation can undermine soil, so it is worth acting the same day rather than waiting for the next business hours.

Signs the leak is in the service line

Unexplained wet ground

A patch that stays damp in dry weather, especially in a straight line from the meter toward the house, is a strong clue.

Meter running with everything off

If the meter or its low-flow indicator moves with all taps, appliances, and irrigation off, water is escaping somewhere on your side of the meter.

Low pressure at every tap

A leak upstream of the house steals pressure from every fixture, not just one. Compare it with how the water ran last month.

A bill that climbs for no clear reason

Slow, steady growth is common with small buried leaks, and a simple meter check can tell you whether the water is leaving through your line.

How we handle a service line repair

1

Confirm the leak is on your side

We check the meter, the shutoff, and the house-side plumbing to separate your line from the utility's side and from fixtures inside the house.

2

Locate the break

We use listening and tracing tools to find the leak, so we dig a small area instead of the whole yard.

3

Explain repair or replacement

A single break in sound pipe is often a spot repair. A line with several failures or aged material is a replacement conversation. You see both options.

4

Call before we dig

Excavation starts only after a USA North 811 ticket, which requires at least two working days of notice. Finding the leak and shutting it off can happen right away.

5

Repair, test, and restore

We make the repair, pressure test it, restore water, and recheck the meter to confirm the line is tight.

What this looks like in Millbrae

Millbrae specifics for service lines

Water and sewer service in Millbrae is provided and maintained by the City of Millbrae Public Works Department, and the water itself comes entirely from the San Francisco Regional Water System through 11 pressure zones. We work on the private side of the line, and if the problem looks like it sits on the city's side, we point you to Public Works.

Pressure matters for a buried line because it is always under load. In a higher-pressure zone, a weak joint or a thinned pipe wall gives out sooner, so we check house-side pressure while the line is open. We cannot map pressure to specific streets, so we measure at your house.

Houses from the 1950s boom, a large share of Millbrae's stock based on Census dwelling counts, often still have their original line from the meter. A failed original line is usually a signal to replace the run instead of patching it a section at a time. We tell you what we find and let you choose.
